## Explicit adjacent-methodology appendix map

These studies remain part of the original intellectual lineage. Their relevance is a possible research lesson, not evidence that their algorithms were transferred into Sentinel-TPD. The main paper should avoid claiming a head-to-head comparison or an implemented method from these sources.

| Original | Actual domain / object of study | Defensible methodological connection | Claim to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10, Shadowcast | Poisoned training examples for vision-language models | Distinguish attack intent, labels and downstream behavioral success. | “Tool shadowing” equivalence or transfer of its poisoning efficacy to MCP. |
| 11–12, Shadow AI | Unsanctioned enterprise use of AI and its governance | Inventory, provenance and control ownership motivate explicit deployment boundaries. | Equating “Shadow AI” with MCP name squatting or citing governance discussion as a tested detector. |
| 14, Cyber Shadows | AI-enabled security threats and policy measures | State organizational assumptions and human/control responsibilities. | Claiming a policy survey validates scanner recall or runtime enforcement. |
| 15, WAFBOOSTER | Mutated malicious web payloads and WAF rules | Use input transformations and benign controls to challenge brittle textual matching. | Claiming the WAF training/boosting method or its measured efficacy was implemented here. |
| 16, SHERPA | Poisoned federated-learning updates and robust aggregation | Keep detector output distinct from defense outcome; articulate adversary access. | Claiming SHAP, clustering, federated aggregation or privacy protection in Sentinel-TPD. |
| 17, Visualizing the Shadows | Demo for visualizing federated-learning poisoning | Record inspectable case-level evidence and distinguish a demonstration from broad evaluation. | Claiming a trained anomaly model or a visual analytical system was transferred into this library. |
| 18, TuDoor | DNS response preprocessing and protocol/state logic bugs | Probe state transitions, malformed inputs and boundary conditions with explicit controls. | Treating DNS exploit success or its threat model as MCP experimental evidence. |
| 19, Docker SoK | Container attack and defense mechanisms | Separate detection from isolation and distinguish a local policy decision from an execution boundary. | Claiming Sentinel-TPD supplies container/process isolation. |
| 20, lung-disease poisoning defense | Image-model training data and medical-image classification | Preserve provenance of inputs and avoid mixing attack detection with downstream model performance. | Claiming watermarks, image transformations, UNet layers or medical efficacy were used in this study. |

Original 13 is an agentic-coding/skills/protocol survey, so it stays in direct ecosystem context while still requiring careful scope. Originals 1–9 are MCP work, but corpus design, model involvement and execution boundaries differ from this study. Metadata confirmation alone is not full-method verification.

## Closest direct work added to the paper

**MCP-Guard** (`mcpguard`) is the published Findings ACL 2026 paper, not an arbitrary similarly named GitHub scanner. Its method has a static-screening stage, a fine-tuned semantic detector and LLM arbitration. The inspected static stage explicitly includes suspicious instruction tags and sensitive-information patterns. Sentinel-TPD cannot claim static metadata pattern screening as a new defense idea. Its measured incremental scope is a pinned local implementation, transparent rule/threshold analysis and reproducible controls; no cross-paper accuracy or speed superiority follows. Verified record: [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.240/).

**MCPShield** (`mcpshield`) means Zhou et al., arXiv:2602.14281v3, a preprint inspected through methods sections 4.1–4.3 and the start of 4.4. It probes tools using metadata-guided mock arguments, judges behavior and maintains trust/history signals; tool-side observation depends on its hosting/instrumentation assumptions. Sentinel-TPD scans selected descriptor text and checks lifecycle fingerprints. These are different measured boundaries, not interchangeable “runtime protection” claims. This is distinct from the later uppercase MCPSHIELD formal-framework paper. [Versioned primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14281v3).

**Semantic Attacks on Tool-Augmented LLMs** (`semanticattacks`) means Jamshidi, Moradi Dakhel, Nafi and Khomh, arXiv:2512.06556v2. The version has four authors and a different title/author configuration from early citations. It studies descriptor manipulation with layered integrity/semantic/runtime defenses. Descriptor integrity and layered defense are prior work; an incremental Sentinel study must characterize what its actual projection and rules cover or miss. Full-method evidence was reused from the earlier verified dossier; no implementation or efficacy was reproduced. [Versioned primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06556v2).

**ETDI, HCP and attested admission** (`etdi`, `hcp`, `attested`) are distinct preprints retained from the previously verified primary-source dossier. HCP expands to **Handle-Capability Protocol**. They motivate identity and execution-control limits, not a change of the current paper into the separate dispatch-conformance study. `mcp2025tools` and `mcp2026tools` are versioned protocol pages; cite the version whose field-level claim is being made.

**Author artifacts**: `sentineltpd` is the pinned subject implementation. `dispatchartifact` is the separate software release *Sentinel Dispatch Conformance: Reproducible Lifecycle Experiments*, v0.1.0, revision e874731f83c31ac0718f70c3c228dd9077928f99. Its release is public software, not a peer-reviewed proceedings publication. Metadata was supplied after the coordinator verified publishing. [Exact release](https://github.com/rajesamp/sentinel-dispatch-conformance/releases/tag/v0.1.0).

## Verification boundary

The dossier verifies all 24 identities at the evidence level specified for each entry, rather than claiming complete metadata or full-method verification. Some publisher pages were unavailable; author-hosted papers, author CVs and institutional records provide explicitly limited evidence. All omitted metadata can be revisited in a later bibliographic pass without changing the empirical result claims. The paper should neither claim new security primitives nor the first analysis of MCP poisoning on the basis of this search.
